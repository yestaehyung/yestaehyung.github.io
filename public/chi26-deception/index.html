"Can LLMs Persuade Humans with Deception?": From a Deceptive Strategy Taxonomy to a Large-Scale Empirical Study

Haein Yeo1, Seungwan Jin1, Taehyung Noh1, Yejin Shin1, Sangyeon Kang1, Sangwoo Heo1, Jiwon Chung1, Hwarim Hyun1, Kyungsik Han1,*
1Hanyang University *Corresponding Author
Paper Code (Coming Soon)

Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) are increasingly used in persuasive contexts — recommendations, negotiations, public discourse — yet little is known about whether and how they rely on deceptive strategies to change human beliefs and behavior. This work addresses that gap in two parts. First, we develop a taxonomy of deceptive persuasion strategies that LLMs can deploy in conversational interactions, grounded in prior literature on rhetoric, manipulation, and misinformation. Second, we conduct a large-scale empirical study in which LLMs attempt to persuade human participants on contested topics, with and without explicit instructions to deceive. We measure which deceptive strategies the models invoke, how those strategies shift participants' beliefs, and how participants detect or fail to detect deception. The findings provide actionable evidence for AI safety: which strategy classes are most influential, where human resistance is brittle, and what kinds of guardrails would meaningfully reduce harmful persuasion without removing legitimate argumentation.

Contributions

Taxonomy

Deceptive Strategy Taxonomy

A structured taxonomy of how LLMs can persuade through deception — covering fabrication, selective omission, misleading framing, false consensus, and emotional manipulation, organized by the cognitive lever each strategy targets.

Empirical Study

Large-Scale Human Study

A controlled, scaled experiment in which LLMs persuade human participants under varying deception conditions, with belief shifts, detection accuracy, and trust calibration measured per strategy class.

AI Safety

Safety Implications

Maps which strategies most effectively bypass human vigilance and where existing safety training fails to suppress them, giving model developers concrete signals about the gaps in alignment for persuasion contexts.

Human-AI Trust

Trust Calibration

Characterizes how participants form, lose, and recover trust under deceptive LLM interaction — informing interface- and policy-level interventions that help users assess AI claims rather than defer to them.
